# Crit 8 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough was realising the brief's two requirements — genuinely
real-time and genuinely multi-user — couldn't be satisfied by inventing a
feature list; they had to come from something actually true in my own life. I
live in BNG, and more than once I've wanted to ask someone to grab something
on their way back, except the one friend I'd actually ask is usually out or
busy. Once I saw that opening that same ask up to the whole building solves
both requirements at once — a runner's post is only real while their trip is
happening, and it only means anything if someone else notices in time to act
on it — the app stopped being an abstract answer to the brief and became
something I could actually picture using myself.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

It changed where I look for ideas. Before this, I tended to start from the
brief and work outward — find a feature that technically satisfies the
requirements, then justify it afterwards. This time the idea came from a
daily annoyance first, and the brief's requirements followed naturally once I
looked at that annoyance honestly. Going forward I want to keep starting from
friction I actually feel, because those are the ideas I can defend without
having to invent reasons for them after the fact.
